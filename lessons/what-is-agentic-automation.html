What is agentic automation?

Learning path: Agentic AIIntermediate8 min readLast updated: October 2026

2-Minute Summary: Agentic automation combines AI-driven interpretation or planning with the dependable execution of workflows, business applications, and people. An agent can handle variable steps; orchestrated automation can validate data, apply controls, and carry out repeatable transactions. Good designs keep permissions narrow, make progress observable, and put human approval where it matters.

What This Means

Agentic automation coordinates flexible AI interpretation with dependable workflows, people, and business systems to achieve a defined outcome.

Simple Explanation

Think of it as a coordinated team of capabilities rather than one all-powerful bot. An agent may understand a request, gather relevant context, or recommend a next action. A workflow handles repeatable business rules and system updates. A person resolves exceptions or approves consequential decisions.

The system should have a defined outcome, explicit boundaries, a way to handle failure, and a record of what happened. Agentic does not mean uncontrolled autonomy: the level of independence should match the risk and evidence that the solution works.

Real-World Example

For a service request, an agent can interpret the message and suggest a category. A workflow validates the requester, retrieves the approved service options, and prepares the case. An employee reviews an exception or confirms a sensitive change before the workflow completes it.

How It Works

A bounded agent can interpret a request or recommend a next step, while a workflow validates data, applies rules, and executes repeatable changes.

Key Takeaways

  • Agentic automation can join interpretation, decisions, execution, and human review.
  • Deterministic controls remain valuable around flexible model behavior.
  • Measure outcomes, exceptions, safety, and cost—not just the number of automated steps.

Common Mistakes

  • Describing a solution as agentic without clarifying what it can decide or do.
  • Leaving ownership, approval, fallback, or audit evidence undefined.
  • Scaling a pilot before testing real exceptions and operational support needs.

Practical Exercise

Draw a simple process with an agent-assisted decision, a deterministic workflow, and a human checkpoint. For each handoff, write the information passed, who owns the next step, what happens on failure, and how you would measure success.
